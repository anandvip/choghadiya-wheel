#Choghadiya · whyTime

Clean = Amrit, Shubh or Labh with no Rahu Kaal, Yamaganda, Gulika, Vaar Vela, Kaal Vela or Kaal Ratri, at least 20 minutes. Partial overlaps are trimmed: the slot is listed as spoiled, and its clean part (20 minutes or more) is listed as a clean window marked trimmed. With the classical eighths an overlay always covers a whole choghadiya, so trimming is rare. Rog, Kaal and Udveg are not for new starts; Chal (neutral) isn’t listed.

    For reference only; calculated, not scraped. Choghadiya is a traditional timing guide, not advice. Times can differ by a minute or two from printed panchangs because of rounding, location and horizon.
    
  
    Method. Sunrise and sunset are calculated in your browser for the apparent upper limb of the Sun with standard refraction (the Drik Panchang convention), using the NOAA/Meeus solar formulas. Day (sunrise→sunset) and night (sunset→next sunrise) are each split into 8 equal choghadiyas, named by the weekday lord in the hora order Sun, Venus, Mercury, Moon, Saturn, Jupiter, Mars → Udveg, Chal, Labh, Amrit, Kaal, Shubh, Rog. Vaar Vela, Kaal Vela (day eighths), Kaal Ratri (night eighths; two on Saturday), Rahu Kaal, Yamaganda and Gulika follow the same eighths. A Vedic day runs sunrise to sunrise, so the hours before sunrise belong to the previous day’s night. Ported from the Swiss-Ephemeris panchang engine and checked against it and Drik Panchang (within 1 minute).
